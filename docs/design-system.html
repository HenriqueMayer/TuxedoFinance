01 / Typography

Typography.

Inter 400–700 is used throughout. Headings use semibold weight, body copy starts at 16px, supporting text starts at 14px, and compact labels start at 12px. The mobile bottom bar is the sole exception: its five complete destination labels use 10–11px, never below 10px, with touch targets of at least 44 × 44px. Narrow screens use tighter spacing rather than truncation or reduced targets. Monetary data keeps tabular numerals.

07 / Layout

Layout & spacing.

A shared workspace width, clear reading groups and labelled scrolling regions keep dense financial content organized.

Workspace width
max-w-7xl mx-auto; px-4 sm:px-6 lg:px-8. Use space-y-6 or space-y-8 between sections.
Cards establish reading groups
Use surface-panel for a bank, chart, input stage or result, and metric-panel for comparable summaries. Keep rows within the group separated by dividers; avoid nesting one card inside another.
Lists and categories
Keep parent and child rows together. Show type and direct usage, then contextual edit actions. Import, export and destructive actions belong in secondary options.
Tables and simulation
Use semantic headers, tabular numeric columns and labelled horizontal scrolling regions. Bank account rows become labelled groups below 768px; planning sheets scroll inside their own region. Change result regions without replacing a focused input. Month overrides are empty until deliberately filled.
Forms
Single-column entry forms use a readable width; related simulation inputs may share rows. Reuse the shared field and choice controls. Keep server errors next to inputs and preserve native submission.
Stable period navigation
Previous and next retain fixed positions. Reset belongs in a reserved second row, so it never replaces or displaces either arrow. Keep unavailable bounds in their own slots.

08 / Motion

Motion.

Functional color and opacity feedback lasts 160–180ms. The public homepage uses a bounded spring for its decorative paper illustration; action contours and text stay fixed, with a small directional arrow response.

Application navigation does not fade the whole interface. HTMX keeps the current server-rendered document visible until the next body is ready, then swaps it immediately; native links remain the no-JavaScript fallback. Reduced motion removes decorative animation while keeping controls usable.

14 / Design decisions

Design decisions.

The approved bow-tie/ledger direction and shared components guide the application. Catalogue examples use synthetic records; their local interactions never save financial data.

Shared components.

Inter, semantic colors, visible labels, five direct workspaces and shared searchable choices. The catalogue also shows bank identity markers, account detail, card debt and conditional entry states; it is a component reference, not a simplified replacement for these distinctions.

Layout and hierarchy.

Fewer nested surfaces, uniform entry fields, fine rules, balanced controls and one graphic signature. Short native choices share a segmented rail. Bank colors identify institutions, never financial status. Synthetic figures are labelled and remain static.

Motion and input.

The bow tie opens a workspace map through painted-area hover, click, touch, Enter or Space. Escape closes it. The date field opens its calendar across the whole control; Tab alone keeps native date editing available. Both interactions retain keyboard access and respect reduced motion. Without JavaScript, dates use the browser calendar and choices remain native.
